# Monotone critical-defect branch

Status: rigorous reductions plus finite exact experiments; not a proof of Collatz.

Use the exact critical-defect coordinate

\[
L=\log_2 3,\qquad r_k=\lfloor(k+1)L\rfloor-\lfloor kL\rfloor\in\{1,2\},
\]
\[
h_k=\lfloor kL\rfloor-A_k,\qquad h_{k+1}=h_k+r_k-a_k.
\]

## 1. Eventual monotonicity forces a one-sided sparse perturbation

If `h_k` is eventually nondecreasing, then `a_k>=1` gives:

- when `r_k=1`, necessarily `a_k=1` and `h_{k+1}=h_k`;
- when `r_k=2`, necessarily `a_k in {1,2}`;
- `h` rises by one exactly at positions with `r_k=2, a_k=1`.

Thus, after the monotone point, the valuation word is obtained from the fixed mechanical word `r` by changing selected symbols `2 -> 1`, and never making the opposite edit.  The number of such edits up to time `n` equals the increase of `h`.

In ordinary Terras parity time, `a_k<=2` means there are never two consecutive even symbols between odd symbols; equivalently the parity word avoids `00` on this tail.

## 2. Summability makes the defect count superlogarithmic

For a divergent nonperiodic orbit, reciprocal/correction summability gives

\[
\sum_n2^{-h_n}<\infty.
\]

Assume now that `h_n` is nondecreasing, integer-valued, and rises by at most one.  Let `ell_m` be the number of indices with `h_n=m`.  Then

\[
\sum_m \ell_m2^{-m}<\infty.
\]

If `tau_{m+1}` is the end of level `m`, then

\[
\frac{\tau_{m+1}}{2^m}
=\sum_{j\le m}(\ell_j2^{-j})2^{j-m}\to0.
\]

Hence uniformly for `n` with `h_n=m`, `n=o(2^m)`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{h_n-\log_2 n\to+\infty.}
\]

So an eventual-monotone counterexample would require infinitely many one-sided defects, growing faster than `log n` (by an unbounded additive margin), while still remaining on the critical-density boundary required for a rational noncyclic orbit.

## 3. Haar rarity and 2-adic dimension

Let

\[
\omega=L-1=\log_2(3/2).
\]

Among the first `n` reference symbols, the number of `r_k=2` positions is exactly

\[
N_2(n)=\lfloor n\omega\rfloor,
\]

and `N_1(n)=n-N_2(n)`.

For a random odd 2-adic seed, a Syracuse valuation letter `a` has cylinder weight `2^{-a}`.  Therefore the total Haar mass of all length-`n` prefixes satisfying the monotone constraint `a_k<=r_k` is

\[
\boxed{
P_n=
(1/2)^{N_1(n)}(3/4)^{N_2(n)}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
-\frac1n\log_2P_n\to
(2-L)+(L-1)\log_2(4/3)
=0.657818872750\ldots
\]

so this branch has Haar measure zero exponentially fast.

Treating the allowed cylinders as a nonstationary Moran construction, its 2-adic Hausdorff dimension `d` is the positive solution of

\[
-d+(L-1)\log_2(1+2^{-d})=0,
\]

namely

\[
\boxed{d\approx0.460883127834.}
\]

This is only a rarity statement: a thin fractal may still contain rational or positive integers.

## 4. Correct finite-prefix seed congruence

For a valuation prefix `(a_0,...,a_{n-1})`, put

\[
A_0=0,\qquad A_j=\sum_{i<j}a_i.
\]

To realize all `n` exponents exactly, it is not enough to work modulo `2^{A_n}`; the endpoint after the prefix must also be odd.  The prefix determines one odd seed residue modulo

\[
\boxed{2^{A_n+1}}.
\]

Using Bernstein's inverse series, the exact class is

\[
\boxed{
N\equiv-
\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{A_j}3^{-j-1}
\pmod{2^{A_n+1}}.
}
\]

This corrects an earlier exploratory calculation that used one power of two too few.

Enumerating all one-sided monotone scripts for short lengths and taking the least positive representative of the exact residue class gives the following record behavior (selected lengths):

These are finite computations only.  Proving that the least positive seed tends to infinity is equivalent to excluding an infinite positive-integer path in this monotone branch, so the table is evidence, not a proof.

A separate family of synthetic near-boundary scripts was generated by targeting

\[
h_n\sim c\log_2n,\qquad c>1,
\]

and raising `h` only at allowed `r_k=2` positions.  At 600 odd steps the exact seed residue typically has essentially the full available bit length `A_n+1`; e.g. for `c=2`, `A_n+1=933` and the least representative has bit length 933.  No stabilization toward a fixed positive integer is observed.

## 6. Real Bernstein sum

For any such monotone branch,

\[
a_k\le r_k,
\]

so `A_j<=floor(jL)`.  The real absolute value of Bernstein's series, when it converges, is

\[
S=\frac13\sum_{j\ge0}2^{A_j-jL}
=\frac13\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-h_j-\{jL\}}.
\]

The maximum possible `h_j` occurs for the all-ones valuation script `a_k=1`, where `A_j=j`.  Therefore

\[
2^{-h_j-\{jL\}}=2^{A_j-jL}\ge(2/3)^j,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{S\ge1.}
\]

Equality occurs only for the all-`a=1` script, whose 2-adic seed is the fixed point `-1`.  Any nontrivial monotone critical script with a convergent real Bernstein series has `S>1`.

This does not by itself exclude a positive integer seed, because real and 2-adic evaluations of the same rational partial sums may converge to different limits.

## 7. Current target

The useful sharpened subproblem is:

> Can a positive integer have an aperiodic parity vector that eventually avoids `00` and whose Syracuse valuation word is a one-sided perturbation `2 -> 1` of the fixed critical mechanical word, with `sum 2^{-h_n}<infinity`?

General bounded-gap / structured-subshift rationality is known to sit directly on the unresolved 2-adic coding bottleneck.  The one-sided critical condition above is considerably narrower and may be attackable via:

- effective lower bounds for exact prefix realizers;
- sparse-perturbation rigidity for the Bernstein--Lagarias conjugacy;
- p-adic transcendence / Subspace-Theorem methods;
- or coupling to the 3-adic reverse barrier.
