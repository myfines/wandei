# GRH diagnostic for the prime-turnover branch

Status: strategic arithmetic diagnosis. Not a Collatz proof and not a conditional proof under GRH.

The sampled mod-9 recurrence gives

3^{q_j}s_j + eta_{j+1} = 2^{t_{j+1}}s_{j+1},

with eta in {3,15,63}. For a two-step recycle of a prime p>7 one obtains

(3/2)^{q_{j+1}} = -2^{d_{j+1}} a_{j+2}/a_{j+1} (mod p),

so multiplicative-order theory for 3/2 mod p is genuinely relevant.

## What GRH would buy

Hooley-type Artin primitive-root results and modern Artin/Chebotarev generalizations show that under suitable GRH assumptions one gets strong density information for primes on which a fixed rational generator has large or maximal multiplicative order. Thus GRH could sharpen the statement that most primes are not small-order primes for 3/2.

## Why GRH does not presently close the branch

The orbit does not sample primes independently from all primes. Its prime divisors are generated by the deterministic sampled cofactors s_j. A density theorem for all primes therefore does not by itself exclude concentration of the orbit on an exceptional prime set.

Moreover, in the bounded-run branch q_j<=Q, for every fixed recycle gap m the block data (T,Q,D) already range over only finitely many possibilities. Any prime recycled across that fixed gap must divide one of finitely many explicit correction integers D_{j,m}. Hence short-gap recycling is already finitely constrained without GRH.

The unresolved case is long-term prime refresh: the orbit may keep introducing genuinely new large primes while avoiding short-gap recycling. GRH does not automatically prevent this deterministic support refresh.

## Conclusion

GRH is a potentially useful conditional tool for a future bad-prime concentration theorem, but it is not the current bottleneck and is not required by the strongest Branch-A argument.

The correct conditional target would be stronger than ordinary Artin density: one would need a theorem that controls the prime divisors of the orbit-generated correction quantities or cofactors themselves, not merely a density statement over all primes.

Therefore the main program remains unconditional:

1. close Branch A using translated finite certificates, correction budget, and continued-fraction geometry;
2. for Branch B, attack deterministic prime-support refresh directly;
3. keep GRH only as a diagnostic side branch, not as a prerequisite.
