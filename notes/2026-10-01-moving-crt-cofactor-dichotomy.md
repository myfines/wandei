# Moving CRT and the cofactor dichotomy

Status: exact consecutive-return congruence plus a computational probe and a proposed number-theoretic split. Not a proof of Collatz.

For consecutive sampled `2 mod 9` returns, use the six-lane induced-map form

\[
M_j+\eta_j=2^{t_j}s_j,
\qquad
M_{j+1}=3^{q_j}s_j,
\]

with

\[
\eta_j\in\{3,15,63\},\qquad s_j\text{ odd}.
\]

The next return also satisfies

\[
M_{j+1}+\eta_{j+1}=2^{t_{j+1}}s_{j+1}.
\]

Hence every pair of consecutive returns obeys the exact moving CRT condition

\[
\boxed{
3^{q_j}s_j\equiv-\eta_{j+1}\pmod{2^{t_{j+1}}}.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
s_j\equiv-\eta_{j+1}3^{-q_j}\pmod{2^{t_{j+1}}}.}
\]

Thus a second long odd-run spike requires the odd cofactor left by the previous spike to land in one very specific residue class modulo a large power of two.

## Small computational probe

For each

\[
\eta\in\{3,15,63\},
\]

and for `t <= 256`, enumerate `1 <= q <= 2t` and compute the least positive odd residue

\[
\rho(q,t,\eta)
:=(-\eta 3^{-q})\bmod 2^t.
\]

This is only a diagnostic experiment, not evidence of a theorem.

At `t=256`, the smallest residues found over `q<=2t` satisfy approximately

\[
\log_2\rho/t\approx
0.9732\quad(\eta=3),
\]

\[
0.9740\quad(\eta=15),
\]

\[
0.9702\quad(\eta=63).
\]

Over the entire tested range `32<=t<=256`, the smallest observed ratios were roughly

\[
0.763,\quad0.670,\quad0.722
\]

for `eta=3,15,63` respectively.

This suggests that comparable consecutive spikes often force `s_j` to be exponentially large in `t_{j+1}`, but no uniform lower bound is claimed.

## Candidate theorem shape

A useful theorem would be any bound of the form

\[
\boxed{
q\le Kt
\Longrightarrow
\rho(q,t,\eta)\ge2^{c_K t-o(t)}
}
\]

for fixed `K` and some `c_K>0`.

Even a weak exponential lower bound would convert consecutive long spikes into an additional height cost beyond the trivial conditions `3^q | M_{j+1}` and `M_{j+1}=-eta mod 2^t`.

## Why p-adic logarithms do not apply immediately

The congruence is not a pure comparison between powers of fixed algebraic numbers: the coefficient `s_j` varies with the orbit. Standard lower bounds for linear forms in p-adic logarithms can control expressions built from a fixed finite multiplicative group, but they do not by themselves give a uniform bound for arbitrary `s_j`.

This leads to a natural dichotomy.

### Arithmetic-simple cofactor branch

If the cofactors `s_j` along an infinite family of spikes lie in a multiplicative group generated by a fixed finite prime set `S`, then

\[
3^{q_j}s_j+\eta_{j+1}=2^{t_{j+1}}s_{j+1}
\]

is an `S`-unit type equation (after adjoining the primes 2,3 and those dividing `eta`). Standard `S`-unit finiteness / p-adic logarithm methods become applicable.

### Arithmetic-complex cofactor branch

If no fixed finite `S` works, then long spikes force continuing refresh of the odd prime support. This is a genuine prime-side necessary condition, not the false claim that Collatz convergence is multiplicative.

The open task is to make this quantitative: relate spike count/size to the number or radical of newly introduced odd primes, then compare that cost with the sampled height and correction constraints.

This moving-CRT/cofactor split is a possible bridge between the induced-map dynamics and analytic/Diophantine number theory.
