# Prime turnover and multiplicative-order clock on sampled cofactors

Status: exact arithmetic consequences of the mod-9 induced map, plus a bounded-run S-unit dichotomy. Not a Collatz proof.

For consecutive sampled `2 mod 9` returns, write

\[
M_j+\eta_j=2^{t_j}s_j,
\qquad
M_{j+1}=3^{q_j}s_j,
\]

with

\[
\eta_j\in\{3,15,63\},\qquad s_j\text{ odd}.
\]

Equivalently the next transition gives

\[
\boxed{
3^{q_j}s_j+\eta_{j+1}=2^{t_{j+1}}s_{j+1}.
}
\]

## 1. Adjacent prime turnover

If an odd prime `p` divides both `s_j` and `s_{j+1}`, then the transition equation implies

\[
p\mid\eta_{j+1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\gcd(s_j,s_{j+1})\mid\eta_{j+1}.}
\]

Since the only prime divisors of `3,15,63` are `3,5,7`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Supp}(s_j)\cap\operatorname{Supp}(s_{j+1})
\subseteq\{3,5,7\}.
}
\]

In particular every prime `p>7` is one-step transient: it cannot divide two consecutive odd cofactors.

## 2. Two-step prime recycling

Compose two transitions:

\[
2^{t_{j+1}+t_{j+2}}s_{j+2}
=3^{q_j+q_{j+1}}s_j+D_j^{(2)},
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
D_j^{(2)}
=3^{q_{j+1}}\eta_{j+1}
+2^{t_{j+1}}\eta_{j+2}.
}
\]

Therefore if `p>7` divides both `s_j` and `s_{j+2}`, then

\[
\boxed{p\mid D_j^{(2)}.}
\]

Using the six-lane normal form `t=q+d` and writing

\[
\eta=3a,\qquad a\in\{1,5,21\},
\]

the condition becomes, modulo `p`,

\[
3^{q_{j+1}}a_{j+1}
+2^{q_{j+1}+d_{j+1}}a_{j+2}\equiv0.
\]

Since `p>7`, all factors are invertible modulo `p`, giving the prime-clock congruence

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac32\right)^{q_{j+1}}
\equiv
-2^{d_{j+1}}\frac{a_{j+2}}{a_{j+1}}
\pmod p.
}
\]

Thus for fixed `p` and fixed adjacent lanes, a two-step recycle forces `q_{j+1}` into a discrete-log residue class modulo

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(3/2).
\]

This is a genuine bridge from the sampled Collatz dynamics to multiplicative-order theory modulo primes.

## 3. General recycling correction

For a gap of `m` sampled returns, define

\[
T_{j,m}=\sum_{r=1}^{m} t_{j+r},
\qquad
Q_{j,m}=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1} q_{j+r}.
\]

Then repeated substitution gives

\[
\boxed{
2^{T_{j,m}}s_{j+m}
=3^{Q_{j,m}}s_j+D_{j,m},
}
\]

where `D_{j,m}>0` depends only on the intermediate sampled block data. One recurrence is

\[
D_{j,1}=\eta_{j+1},
\]

\[
D_{j,m+1}
=3^{q_{j+m}}D_{j,m}
+2^{T_{j,m}}\eta_{j+m+1}.
\]

Hence every prime `p>7` which appears in both endpoint cofactors must satisfy

\[
\boxed{p\mid D_{j,m}.}
\]

So recycled large primes are divisors of explicit finite sums of `2,3`-S-units with digits from `{3,15,63}`.

## 4. Bounded-run branch: smooth cofactors have zero density

Assume the sampled odd-run counts are uniformly bounded:

\[
q_j\le Q\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j.
\]

Fix a prime threshold `P` and call an integer `P`-smooth if all its prime factors are at most `P`.

Fix a gap `m`. Because

- `q_j` lies in the finite set `{0,...,Q}`;
- every lane `d_j` lies in `{1,...,6}`;
- `t_j=q_j+d_j`;
- every `eta_j` lies in `{3,15,63}`;

there are only finitely many possible triples

\[
(T_{j,m},Q_{j,m},D_{j,m})
\]

for this fixed `m`.

If both endpoint cofactors `s_j,s_{j+m}` are `P`-smooth, then

\[
2^T s_{j+m}-3^R s_j=D
\]

for one of finitely many fixed nonzero `D`. After dividing by `D`, this is an `S`-unit equation

\[
x+y=1
\]

for a fixed finite prime set `S` consisting of primes at most `P` together with primes dividing `2*3*D`.

By the classical `S`-unit finiteness theorem, each fixed template has only finitely many endpoint solutions. Since an unbounded deterministic orbit cannot repeat sampled states indefinitely, for each fixed gap `m` only finitely many pairs of `P`-smooth cofactors can occur at distance `m`.

Consequently, for every fixed `L`, after some point no two `P`-smooth cofactors occur within `L` sampled returns of one another. Therefore their upper density is zero:

\[
\boxed{
q_j\le Q\text{ eventually}
\Longrightarrow
\forall P,
\quad
\{j:s_j\text{ is }P\text{-smooth}\}
\text{ has density }0.
}
\]

Equivalently, in the bounded-run branch the largest prime factor of `s_j` tends to infinity in density:

\[
\boxed{
P^+(s_j)\to\infty
\quad\text{in density.}
}
\]

This yields a clean structural dichotomy for any hypothetical sampled escape tail:

1. **unbounded spike branch:** `q_j` is unbounded, producing arbitrarily long odd runs and the known high `2`-adic / `3`-adic alignment;
2. **prime-refresh branch:** `q_j` is bounded, but then cofactors must be increasingly non-smooth, with large odd prime factors refreshed on a density-one set of sampled times.

## 5. Relation to multiplicative-order results

The two-step recycling congruence shows why multiplicative-order theory is relevant. For fixed `p>7`, recycling after one missing cofactor requires a discrete logarithm in the cyclic subgroup generated by `3/2 mod p`.

Classical Erdős--Murty/Pappalardi-type results show that multiplicative orders of fixed generators are large for almost all primes (roughly square-root scale unconditionally, substantially stronger under GRH in related formulations). However this does not by itself solve the Collatz problem: the prime divisors generated by a hypothetical orbit are highly non-random and could in principle concentrate on the exceptional small-order primes.

A possible next theorem is therefore a **bad-prime concentration exclusion**: show that a sampled orbit cannot refresh its cofactor support almost entirely from primes for which `ord_p(3/2)` is anomalously small, while still satisfying the correction-summability and critical-drift constraints.
